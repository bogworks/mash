Policy apply hardening: a rotation that is submitted again resumes instead of failing as a duplicate key, a reissue of an active key is recorded as a new revision and a revoked key is refused inside the record write itself, and a change that needs an old-key inventory is refused unless the issue record supplies a complete one. A pending generation whose new inbox was created is no longer rolled back while the inbox can still be reached, for ledgers that keep provisioning markers.

A retried rotation now revokes, and reports, the old keys again even when an earlier attempt already marked them revoked, so the revocations are in the account JWT the retry commits. The old-key inventory takes each key's greatest issue time over its whole retained history (a future-dated earlier credential included) and is refused when that history is incomplete or at the bucket's cap. Apply refuses a Provisioner when no provisioning markers are kept (`ErrNoProvisionMarkers`; set `ApplyDeps.ProvisionMarkers`), before anything is pushed.

A removed sender's keys are handed to the revoker again on a retry, as a rotation's are, and a resumed rotation records the issue time of the credential it delivers. A reissue cannot move an active key between the sender and probe classes (`ErrDuplicateKey`), which would hide a live sender credential from the inventory. A provisioning marker that cannot be read, or has no address, now fails `Provisioning` instead of being skipped, and `RunOrdered` itself refuses a Provisioner with a ledger that keeps no provisioning markers.
